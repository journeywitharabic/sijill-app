Your child’s page — a short guide

Qur’an School · مَدْرَسَةُ القُرْآنِ · five minutes to read, once.

Every family has a private link that opens one page: what your child is working on, how they are getting on, and what to practise at home. It updates itself — there is nothing to install and nothing to log into.

Open it after every class. Homework is set at the end of each lesson and is due at the next one, so looking the same evening or the day after lets you plan the week and split the work into short daily sessions instead of one long one the night before.

Section by section

1
English or Arabic, light or dark The two buttons in the top corner. العربية turns the whole page into Arabic — and back again, where it then reads English. switches between the light page and a dark one, easier on the eyes at night. The page remembers both on that phone, so you choose once.
2
Your children If you have more than one child at the school, they appear as buttons here. Tap a name to switch. One link covers your whole family.
3
Name and group We show first name and last initial only. Your child’s full name, your phone number and your email are not stored in this app at all — see the note on privacy at the end.
4
How it is going One arrow, reading the last few classes together — not the last one. It looks at whether new ground is being covered and whether what was learned before is holding. It is the quickest thing on the page: if the arrow is green, carry on as you are.
moving forward New ground is going in and the review list is not growing. standing still Coming to class, but little is being added. Usually the week between classes. needs attention Ground is being lost — the review list is growing faster than the new work. Start with section 9. not enough to say Too few classes so far, or too many missed, to call it either way. Normal in September.
Your child’s teacher sees the same arrow, worked out the same way, so the two of you are never looking at different answers.
5
Still to be heard Work your child was given at an earlier class that their teacher has not yet had the chance to listen to. It is still worth practising — it has not gone away, and it will come round. This card only appears when there is something in it.
6
To practise for the next class What was set at the last class, with the day it is due back. Memorize is new ground. Review is something learned before that is coming back round. Each line says when it was set and by whom. Homework is set even when your child was absent, so this is the place to look after a missed week.
7
In class What the whole class did together that day, written by the teacher who taught it — the group revision, the tajwīd point everyone worked on. It is about the class, not about your child, so it never names anyone. The last three appear here, newest first.
8
Attendance One square per class, most recent last, then the totals for the year. The key underneath names all six states. Two of them are easy to mix up: a hatched square means the school closed that day and it counts against nobody; an empty dashed square means the class ran but your child was not marked — if you see several of those, tell us.
9
Where your child is How much of the current juz is solid, how much still needs review. Pages are a fairer measure than surahs, because surahs vary enormously in length. This fills in after the teacher has done the first assessment — usually in the first few weeks.
10
Needs review — help at home The surahs that have slipped. This is the most useful list on the page: ten minutes on one of these during the week is worth more than an hour the night before.
11
Recent classes What actually happened, newest first — what was recited, and the teacher who heard it. Three things can appear here:

Tajwīd stars

Beside each passage, out of five, for the recitation itself — makārij, madd, the rules being applied. It is graded against your child’s own level.

How each passage went

Beside what was recited you may see good, average, again or not ready. Average means four or five mistakes: it counts, your child knows the passage, and it will simply come round for review sooner than one marked good. Again means it comes back next week. Not ready means they were in class but had not worked on it.

Adab — behaviour

Out of five, with a face. Most weeks this will not appear at all — it is only recorded when a teacher has something to tell you, good or otherwise. The lowest mark cannot be given without a written line explaining it, so you will never receive a bare number with no context.

Lines without a recitation

Class closed — no class that day; nothing is counted. Here — nothing recorded — your child attended but did not recite. Not marked — the class ran and no attendance was taken for your child.

Keep the link handy

The page lives at a web address, so put it where you will find it again:

  • iPhone / iPad (Safari) — open the link, tap Share (the square with the arrow), then Add to Home Screen. It behaves like an app.
  • Android (Chrome) — open the link, tap the ⋮ menu, then Add to Home screen.
  • Or simply bookmark it, or keep our ClassDojo message so you can tap the link again.
Your link is private — please do not forward it. Anyone holding it can see your child’s page. If you think it has gone astray, tell us and we will issue a new one; the old one stops working immediately.

What the teachers do

So you know where this comes from. In class the teacher marks who is here, listens to each child, and records what was recited and how it went. Setting next week’s homework is part of the same few taps. Nothing is written about your child that a teacher would not say to you directly — that is the rule we work to.

Privacy

The app holds first name and last initial, the class, attendance, what was recited, and homework. It holds no parent names, no phone numbers, no email addresses, no dates of birth and no addresses. Nothing identifies your family publicly. Messages still go through ClassDojo as they always have.
